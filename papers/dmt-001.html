Regularity-Selected Generic Rank at Least Four
in the Piezas Diophantine Sextuple

C. R. Hill · September 12, 2026

Abstract. We study the one-parameter Piezas rational Diophantine sextuple over ℚ(t). Its regularity identities select six of the twenty triple classes; for each selected class, four explicit rational sections are proved independent by specialization at t = 12/5 using rigorous canonical-height Gram certificates, yielding generic Mordell–Weil rank at least four.

1. Introduction

A rational Diophantine \(m\)-tuple is a set of distinct nonzero rational numbers such that the product of any two distinct elements, increased by \(1\), is a rational square. Rational Diophantine sextuples exist in infinite families [1]. The particularly simple one-parameter sextuple used here was given by Piezas [2] and subsequently studied by Dujella and Kazalicki [3].

A rational Diophantine triple \(\{u,v,w\}\) induces an elliptic curve \[Y^2=(uX+1)(vX+1)(wX+1).\] The use of rational Diophantine triples to construct elliptic curves of high rank has a substantial history. Dujella constructed induced families of generic rank at least four [4]; Aguirre, Dujella, and Peral later obtained generic rank at least five [5]; and Dujella and Peral constructed a family of generic rank at least seven [6]. Thus generic rank at least four is not itself the new feature of the present result.

Regular substructures inside rational Diophantine sextuples have also been studied previously. Dujella, Kazalicki, and Petričević constructed infinite families containing regular quadruples and regular quintuples [7]. More recently, Zeng studied regular extensions and compatibility for standard parametric rational Diophantine sextuples over \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\) [8].

The contribution here is the link between the internal regularity structure of the Piezas sextuple and the ranks of elliptic curves induced by its three-element subfamilies. The regularity identities themselves are not claimed as new. What is proved here is that their incidence pattern selects six specific triple classes from the twenty available three-element subfamilies, and that each selected class carries four independent rational sections over \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\). Thus the theorem gives a regularity-indexed generic rank lower bound for these six induced elliptic-curve families.

The proof has two parts. First, five exact regularity identities produce a small incidence graph that selects six distinguished triples. Second, each selected triple is shown to carry four rational sections. Their independence is certified at the common good specialization \[t=\frac{12}{5}\] by positive-definite canonical-height Gram matrices. Only persistence of a section relation under good specialization is used; injectivity of the full specialization homomorphism is not required.

2. The Piezas family and its regularity graph

Let \[\begin{align*} a={}&\frac{(t^2-2t-1)(t^2+2t+3)(3t^2-2t+1)}{4t(t^2-1)(t^2+2t-1)},\\ b={}&\frac{4t(t^2-1)(t^2-2t-1)}{(t^2+2t-1)^3},\\ c={}&\frac{4t(t^2-1)(t^2+2t-1)}{(t^2-2t-1)^3},\\ d={}&\frac{(t^2+2t-1)(t^2-2t+3)(3t^2+2t+1)}{4t(t^2-1)(t^2-2t-1)},\\ e={}&\frac{-t(t^2+4t+1)(t^2-4t+1)}{(t-1)(t+1)(t^2+2t-1)(t^2-2t-1)},\\ f={}&\frac{(t-1)(t+1)(3t^2-1)(t^2-3)}{4t(t^2+2t-1)(t^2-2t-1)}. \end{align*}\] These functions form the Piezas rational Diophantine sextuple over \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\) [3]. Thus for every distinct \(i,j\in\{a,b,c,d,e,f\}\) there exists \(\rho_{ij}\in\mathbb{Q}(t)\) satisfying \[\rho_{ij}^2=ij+1.\] We choose one such square root for each unordered pair and keep that choice fixed.

We use the standard algebraic regularity tests [7]. An ordered quadruple \((p,q,r,s)\) is regular when \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:r4} (p-q-r+s)^2=4(ps+1)(qr+1), \end{equation}\] and an ordered quintuple \((p,q,r,s,z)\) is regular when \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:r5} (pqrsz+2pqr+p+q+r-s-z)^2 =4(pq+1)(pr+1)(qr+1)(sz+1). \end{equation}\] These identities are convenient here because they can be checked directly in \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\); no specialization is involved.

Proposition 1. (Regularity pattern). For the Piezas family, the identities (1) and (2) hold identically in \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\) for the ordered quadruples \[(a,b,e,f),\qquad (a,d,e,f),\qquad (c,d,e,f),\] and the ordered quintuples \[(a,b,c,d,e),\qquad (a,b,c,d,f).\]

Proof. The claim is an exact rational-function calculation. Substitute the six displayed Piezas functions into the left side minus the right side of (1) for \[(a,b,e,f),\qquad (a,d,e,f),\qquad (c,d,e,f),\] and into the corresponding residual of (2) for \[(a,b,c,d,e),\qquad (a,b,c,d,f).\] After cancellation in the fraction field \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\), all five residuals are zero. ∎

For the combinatorial argument, we now forget the ordering and retain only the underlying label sets. The three regular quadruples share the pair \[\{e,f\}.\] Removing that pair leaves the two-element sets \[\{a,b\},\qquad \{a,d\},\qquad \{c,d\},\] which we regard as edges on the vertex set \(\{a,b,c,d\}\). Thus the resulting graph is the path \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:path} c-d-a-b. \end{equation}\] Its endpoints are \(b,c\), and its distance-two pairs are \(ac,bd\).

Definition 2. (Regularity-selected triples). The regularity-selected triples are obtained in either of two ways:

  1. take the special pair \(\{e,f\}\) together with an endpoint of (3);

  2. take one element of \(\{e,f\}\) together with a distance-two pair of (3).

Thus \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:selected} \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}}=\{ace,acf,bde,bdf,bef,cef\}. \end{equation}\]

There are exactly six such triples: two of the first type and four of the second. This is a selection rule coming from the regularity graph. It does not by itself say anything about the ranks of the other fourteen triple classes.

3. Four sections on each selected curve

For a Diophantine triple \(T=\{u,v,w\}\), we use the standard equivalent model for elliptic curves induced by Diophantine triples [9], \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:Euvw} E_T:\qquad y^2=(x+uv)(x+uw)(x+vw). \end{equation}\] It is obtained from \[Y^2=(uX+1)(vX+1)(wX+1)\] by the change of variables \[x=uvwX,\qquad y=uvwY.\] In the six cases considered below, \(u,v,w\) are nonzero and pairwise distinct as rational functions, so (5) is nonsingular over \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\).

Write \[r=\rho_{uv},\qquad s=\rho_{uw},\qquad q=\rho_{vw},\] so that \[r^2=uv+1,\qquad s^2=uw+1,\qquad q^2=vw+1.\]

The standard Diophantine-triple construction gives the rational point [9] \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:P} P_T=(0,uvw). \end{equation}\] The same construction gives \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:R} R_T=(rs+rq+sq+1,(r+s)(r+q)(s+q)). \end{equation}\] Direct substitution using \[r^2=uv+1,\qquad s^2=uw+1,\qquad q^2=vw+1\] verifies that \[R_T\in E_T(\mathbb{Q}(t)).\]

Every fourth element \(z\) from the ambient sextuple gives another rational point \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:Ez} E_z= \bigl( uvwz,\, uvw\rho_{uz}\rho_{vz}\rho_{wz} \bigr) \in E_T(\mathbb{Q}(t)). \end{equation}\] Indeed, substitution into (5) gives \[(uz+1)(vz+1)(wz+1) = \rho_{uz}^2\rho_{vz}^2\rho_{wz}^2.\]

For the six selected triples we use the following four sections.

\(T\) four sections
\(ace\) \(P_T,\ R_T,\ E_d,\ E_f\)
\(acf\) \(P_T,\ R_T,\ E_d,\ E_e\)
\(bde\) \(P_T,\ R_T,\ E_c,\ E_f\)
\(bdf\) \(P_T,\ R_T,\ E_c,\ E_e\)
\(bef\) \(P_T,\ R_T,\ E_c,\ E_d\)
\(cef\) \(P_T,\ R_T,\ E_a,\ E_b\)

Proposition 3. (Section identities). For every \(T\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}}\), the four sections listed above lie in \(E_T(\mathbb{Q}(t))\).

Proof. The point \(P_T\) satisfies (5) immediately.

For \(R_T\), substitute (7) into (5) and use \[r^2=uv+1,\qquad s^2=uw+1,\qquad q^2=vw+1.\] The resulting residual is zero.

For \(E_z\), equation (8) and the identities \[\rho_{uz}^2=uz+1,\qquad \rho_{vz}^2=vz+1,\qquad \rho_{wz}^2=wz+1\] give the curve equation directly.

Thus all four listed sections have coordinates in \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\) and satisfy (5) identically. ∎

4. Independence from one good specialization

It remains to prove that the four sections chosen for each of the six selected families are independent over \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\). We do this at one common good specialization.

Lemma 4. (Relation persistence). Let \(E/\mathbb{Q}(t)\) be an elliptic curve and let \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_m\in E(\mathbb{Q}(t))\). Suppose that \(t_0\in\mathbb{Q}\) is a value at which \(E\) specializes to a nonsingular elliptic curve and all \(Q_i\) specialize to finite rational points. If \[Q_1(t_0),\ldots,Q_m(t_0)\] are independent in \(E_{t_0}(\mathbb{Q})\), then \[Q_1,\ldots,Q_m\] are independent in \(E(\mathbb{Q}(t))\).

Proof. Any integral relation \[n_1Q_1+\cdots+n_mQ_m=O\] among the generic sections specializes at a good value \(t_0\) to \[n_1Q_1(t_0)+\cdots+n_mQ_m(t_0)=O.\] If the specialized points are independent, every \(n_i\) must be zero. ∎

We use the common specialization \[t_0=\frac{12}{5}.\] For each \[T\in\{ace,acf,bde,bdf,bef,cef\},\] the specialized curve is nonsingular and the four sections listed in Section 3 specialize to finite rational points.

Their exact coordinates and the specialized curves are recorded in Appendix A. Appendix B gives rigorous interval enclosures for the complete \(4\times4\) canonical-height Gram matrix of each four-point set. The certification method is described in Appendix C.

For points \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_4\), write \[G_T= \bigl(\langle Q_i,Q_j\rangle\bigr)_{1\le i,j\le4},\] where \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\) is the Néron–Tate pairing. The interval-Cholesky certificate in Appendix B has four strictly positive pivot intervals for every selected class. Hence each exact matrix \(G_T\) is positive definite.

Proposition 5. (Specialized independence). At \(t=12/5\), the four selected points are independent on each of the six curves \[E_{ace},\ E_{acf},\ E_{bde},\ E_{bdf},\ E_{bef},\ E_{cef}.\]

Proof. For each curve, Appendix B certifies that the exact canonical-height Gram matrix \(G_T\) is positive definite. Therefore, for every nonzero \(n\in\mathbb{Z}^4\), \[\widehat h\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^4 n_iQ_i\right) = n^{\mathsf T}G_Tn >0.\] Thus no nontrivial integral relation exists among the four specialized points. ∎

By Lemma 4, the corresponding four sections are therefore independent over \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\).

This argument uses specialization only to rule out relations among the four explicitly constructed sections. It does not require injectivity of the full specialization homomorphism.

5. Main theorem

The regularity graph selects six triple classes from the Piezas sextuple. For each selected class, Section 3 constructs four rational sections, and Section 4 proves their independence by specialization.

Theorem 6. (Regularity-selected generic rank). Let \(a,b,c,d,e,f\in\mathbb{Q}(t)\) be the Piezas sextuple defined above. For every \[T\in\{ace,acf,bde,bdf,bef,cef\},\] let \(E_T/\mathbb{Q}(t)\) be the elliptic curve induced by \(T\). Then \[\operatorname{rank}E_T(\mathbb{Q}(t))\ge 4.\] More precisely, the four sections listed in Section 3 are independent in \(E_T(\mathbb{Q}(t))\).

Proof. Proposition 3 gives four rational sections on each selected curve. Proposition 5 proves that these four sections specialize to independent rational points at the common good value \[t=\frac{12}{5}.\] By Lemma 4, any integral relation among the generic sections would specialize to the same relation among those four specialized points. No such nontrivial relation exists. Therefore the four generic sections are independent, and \[\operatorname{rank}E_T(\mathbb{Q}(t))\ge 4\] for every selected triple \(T\). ∎

Corollary 7.. The regularity pattern determined by the special pair \(\{e,f\}\) and the path \[c-d-a-b\] selects six induced elliptic-curve families, each with generic rank at least four.

Scope.

The theorem proves a generic rank lower bound for the six selected families. It does not prove that their generic rank is exactly four. It gives no rank upper bound for the remaining fourteen triple classes, and it does not assert that the regularity-selection condition is necessary for generic rank at least four.

6. Discussion

The result here is structural. The six rank \(\ge4\) families are not identified by a rank search alone. They are selected first by the regularity pattern of the parent sextuple, and the rank lower bound is proved afterward. That order matters.

The three regular quadruples share the pair \(\{e,f\}\). Removing that pair leaves the path \[c-d-a-b.\] Its endpoints and distance-two pairs select \[ace,\ acf,\ bde,\ bdf,\ bef,\ cef.\] For each of these six selected triples, the theorem proves four independent rational sections over \(\mathbb{Q}(t)\).

The height calculation is used only to certify independence at one good specialization. It does not choose the six families. The selection comes from the regularity structure itself. This separates the structural part of the argument from the rank certification step.

This proves the forward direction only. The six selected families have generic rank at least four. The theorem does not show that their generic rank is exactly four, and it does not show that the other fourteen triple classes have generic rank at most three. It also does not show that the regularity rule is necessary for generic rank at least four. Additional independent sections may exist on either side of the split.

The next test is to leave the Piezas family. If another parametric rational Diophantine sextuple has a different internal regularity pattern, does that pattern select a different rank stratification? If so, then the induced elliptic curves retain information from the parent Diophantine structure that is lost when the curves are studied one at a time. The present theorem proves one concrete case of that mechanism.

Appendix C. Canonical-height certification method

This appendix describes the rigorous height enclosure used in Appendix B. The purpose is to identify the numerical step that carries the independence proof and to state the bounds used there.

Short-Weierstrass certification model

Write a specialized curve in generalized Weierstrass form \[E:\qquad y^2+a_1xy+a_3y = x^3+a_2x^2+a_4x+a_6.\] Let \(b_2,b_4,b_6,b_8,c_4,c_6\) denote the standard Weierstrass invariants. The exact change of variables \[X=36x+3b_2, \qquad Y=216y+108a_1x+108a_3\] maps \(E\) to the short Weierstrass model \[E^{\mathrm{sh}}:\qquad Y^2=X^3+AX+B,\] where \[A=-27c_4, \qquad B=-54c_6.\] This model is used only for the height certificate. It is \(\mathbb{Q}\)-isomorphic to the original curve.

For a projective \(x\)-coordinate \((X:Z)\), define \[h_x(X:Z)=\log\max\{|X|,|Z|\},\] after replacing \((X,Z)\) by a primitive integral pair.

On the short model, duplication of the \(x\)-coordinate is represented by the homogeneous quartic forms \[\begin{align} F(X,Z) &= X^4-2AX^2Z^2-8BXZ^3+A^2Z^4, \label{eq:dup-F} \\ G(X,Z) &= 4X^3Z+4AXZ^3+4BZ^4. \label{eq:dup-G} \end{align}\] After clearing denominators and removing common content, \(F\) and \(G\) are taken to be primitive integral forms.

A uniform duplication-height bound

The height error is controlled by exact Bézout certificates for the two affine charts of the duplication map.

Define \[f_x(T)=F(1,T), \qquad g_x(T)=G(1,T),\] and \[f_z(T)=F(T,1), \qquad g_z(T)=G(T,1).\] Thus the \(x\)-chart is used when \(|X|\ge |Z|\), with \(T=Z/X\), and the \(z\)-chart is used when \(|Z|\ge |X|\), with \(T=X/Z\).

Exact extended Euclidean calculations give integer polynomials \(s_x,t_x,s_z,t_z\) and nonzero integers \(c_x,c_z\) satisfying \[s_xf_x+t_xg_x=c_x, \qquad s_zf_z+t_zg_z=c_z.\] Put \[K_x=\|s_x\|_1+\|t_x\|_1, \qquad K_z=\|s_z\|_1+\|t_z\|_1,\] and \[C_{\mathrm{up}} = \max\{1,\|F\|_1,\|G\|_1\}.\] The certification constant is \[\begin{equation} C_E= \max\{ 1,\, C_{\mathrm{up}},\, K_z|c_x|,\, K_x|c_z| \}. \label{eq:CE} \end{equation}\] All quantities entering (11) are obtained with exact integer arithmetic.

Lemma 9. (Uniform duplication-height bound). For every rational point \(Q\) on the short certification model, \[\begin{equation} \left| h_x(2Q)-4h_x(Q) \right| \le \log C_E. \label{eq:dup-height-bound} \end{equation}\]

Proof. Let \((X,Z)\) be the primitive integral projective \(x\)-coordinate of \(Q\), and put \[M=\max\{|X|,|Z|\}.\] Write \[F_Q=F(X,Z), \qquad G_Q=G(X,Z), \qquad d=\gcd(|F_Q|,|G_Q|).\] The duplication forms have no common projective zero on a smooth elliptic curve, so \(F_Q\) and \(G_Q\) are not both zero. The primitive projective \(x\)-coordinate of \(2Q\) is therefore \[\left( \frac{F_Q}{d}:\frac{G_Q}{d} \right).\]

For the upper bound, the coefficient norms give \[|F_Q|\le \|F\|_1M^4, \qquad |G_Q|\le \|G\|_1M^4.\] Since \(d\ge1\), \[\max\left\{ \left|\frac{F_Q}{d}\right|, \left|\frac{G_Q}{d}\right| \right\} \le C_{\mathrm{up}}M^4 \le C_EM^4.\] Taking logarithms gives \[h_x(2Q)-4h_x(Q)\le\log C_E.\]

For the reverse bound, first control the common divisor \(d\). Homogenizing the two Bézout identities gives identities of the form \[S_x(X,Z)F(X,Z)+T_x(X,Z)G(X,Z) = c_xX^{N_x},\] and \[S_z(X,Z)F(X,Z)+T_z(X,Z)G(X,Z) = c_zZ^{N_z},\] for suitable homogeneous integer polynomials \(S_x,T_x,S_z,T_z\) and positive integers \(N_x,N_z\). Since \(d\) divides both \(F_Q\) and \(G_Q\), \[d\mid c_xX^{N_x}, \qquad d\mid c_zZ^{N_z}.\] Because \((X,Z)\) is primitive, \[\gcd(X,Z)=1.\] Prime by prime, the two divisibilities therefore imply \[d\mid c_xc_z,\] and hence \[d\le |c_xc_z|.\]

Suppose first that \(|X|=M\). Then \(|Z/X|\le1\). Evaluating the \(x\)-chart Bézout identity at \(T=Z/X\) gives \[|c_x| \le \left( |s_x(Z/X)|+|t_x(Z/X)| \right) \max\left\{ |f_x(Z/X)|,\, |g_x(Z/X)| \right\}.\] Since \(|Z/X|\le1\), \[|s_x(Z/X)|+|t_x(Z/X)|\le K_x.\] Using \[F_Q=X^4f_x(Z/X), \qquad G_Q=X^4g_x(Z/X),\] we obtain \[|c_x|M^4 \le K_x\max\{|F_Q|,|G_Q|\}.\] After division by \(d\) and using \(d\le|c_xc_z|\), \[\max\left\{ \left|\frac{F_Q}{d}\right|, \left|\frac{G_Q}{d}\right| \right\} \ge \frac{M^4}{K_x|c_z|}.\]

If instead \(|Z|=M\), the same argument on the reciprocal chart gives \[\max\left\{ \left|\frac{F_Q}{d}\right|, \left|\frac{G_Q}{d}\right| \right\} \ge \frac{M^4}{K_z|c_x|}.\] Therefore, in both cases, \[M^4 \le \max\{K_z|c_x|,K_x|c_z|\} \max\left\{ \left|\frac{F_Q}{d}\right|, \left|\frac{G_Q}{d}\right| \right\}.\] By (11), \[4h_x(Q)-h_x(2Q)\le\log C_E.\] Combining the two bounds proves (12). ∎

We use the standard Néron–Tate canonical height and associated bilinear pairing [10]. With the normalization used here, \[\widehat h(Q) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{h_x(2^nQ)}{2\cdot4^n}.\] Applying Lemma 9 along the duplication tower gives \[\begin{equation} \left| \widehat h(Q) - \frac{h_x(2^nQ)}{2\cdot4^n} \right| \le \frac{\log C_E}{6\cdot4^n}. \label{eq:height-tail} \end{equation}\] Indeed, \[\begin{aligned} \left| \widehat h(Q) - \frac{h_x(2^nQ)}{2\cdot4^n} \right| &\le \sum_{k=n}^{\infty} \frac{ \left| h_x(2^{k+1}Q)-4h_x(2^kQ) \right| }{ 2\cdot4^{k+1} } \\ &\le \frac{\log C_E}{2} \sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4^{k+1}} \\ &= \frac{\log C_E}{6\cdot4^n}. \end{aligned}\]

For all six specialized curves, the maximum in (11) is attained by the same term: \[C_E=K_x|c_z|.\] The resulting exact integers are large, ranging from \(195\) to \(275\) decimal digits. Their sizes are:

The duplication tower itself is computed in exact rational/projective arithmetic. Floating-point arithmetic is used only to represent interval endpoints, and every elementary interval operation is rounded outward.

Pairing intervals

For rational points \(Q_i,Q_j\), polarization gives \[\langle Q_i,Q_j\rangle = \frac{ \widehat h(Q_i+Q_j) -\widehat h(Q_i) -\widehat h(Q_j) }{2}.\] If \[I_h(Q_i)=[a_i,b_i], \qquad I_h(Q_j)=[a_j,b_j], \qquad I_h(Q_i+Q_j)=[a_{ij},b_{ij}],\] then an enclosing interval for the pairing is \[\begin{equation} I_{ij} = \left[ \frac{a_{ij}-b_i-b_j}{2}, \, \frac{b_{ij}-a_i-a_j}{2} \right]. \label{eq:pairing-interval} \end{equation}\] The diagonal entries are the intervals \(I_h(Q_i)\). Applying (14) and (15) to the four points on each curve produces the six interval matrices displayed in Appendix B.

Interval Cholesky certificate

Let \[\mathcal G=(I_{ij})\] be one of the symmetric interval matrices in Appendix B. Fixed-order interval Cholesky is performed with outward-rounded interval arithmetic.

At step \(i\), suppose the preceding interval entries of the lower-triangular factor have been constructed. The next squared diagonal entry is enclosed by \[D_i = I_{ii} - \sum_{m<i}L_{im}^2.\] If \[\inf D_i>0,\] then \(\sqrt{D_i}\) is defined and has strictly positive lower endpoint. The off-diagonal entries in column \(i\) are then enclosed by \[L_{ji} = \frac{ I_{ji}-\sum_{m<i}L_{jm}L_{im} }{ \sqrt{D_i} }, \qquad j>i.\]

Lemma 10. (Interval Cholesky certificate). Suppose the fixed-order interval Cholesky calculation for a symmetric interval matrix \(\mathcal G\) produces pivot intervals \[D_1,\ldots,D_m\] such that \[\inf D_i>0 \qquad \text{for every }i.\] Then every exact real symmetric matrix represented by \(\mathcal G\) is positive definite.

Proof. Let \(G\) be an exact real symmetric matrix represented by \(\mathcal G\). Apply the ordinary Cholesky recursion to \(G\) in the same fixed order.

At the first step, the exact first squared diagonal entry lies in \(D_1\). Since \[\inf D_1>0,\] it is positive. Its square root therefore exists and lies in \(\sqrt{D_1}\). Because the lower endpoint of \(\sqrt{D_1}\) is strictly positive, the divisions defining the remaining entries in the first Cholesky column are valid. Outward-rounded interval arithmetic encloses all of those exact entries.

Assume inductively that the exact Cholesky entries through column \(i-1\) lie in the intervals already constructed. Interval addition, subtraction, multiplication, squaring, square root, and division preserve inclusion whenever the denominator interval excludes zero. It follows that the exact \(i\)th squared diagonal entry lies in \(D_i\). Since \[\inf D_i>0,\] that entry is positive, its square root lies in \(\sqrt{D_i}\), and \(\sqrt{D_i}\) is bounded away from zero. The exact off-diagonal entries in column \(i\) therefore lie in the intervals produced by the stated recursion.

The induction continues through all columns. Every exact Cholesky diagonal entry of \(G\) is positive. Hence \(G\) admits a Cholesky factorization with positive diagonal and is positive definite. ∎

The six certificates in Appendix B have strictly positive lower endpoints at all four Cholesky steps. By Lemma 10, the exact canonical-height Gram matrix \(G_T\) is positive definite for each of the six specialized curves. Therefore \[n^{\mathsf T}G_Tn>0 \qquad \text{for every }0\ne n\in\mathbb Z^4,\] which proves independence of the four specialized points.

Logical role of the computation

The certified computation is used only at the specialization \[t=\frac{12}{5}.\] The generic section identities are exact identities in \(\mathbb Q(t)\) and do not depend on numerical height calculations.

Suppose, for one of the six selected families, that the four generic sections satisfied a nontrivial relation \[n_1Q_1+n_2Q_2+n_3Q_3+n_4Q_4=O, \qquad (n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)\ne(0,0,0,0).\] All four sections and the curve have good specialization at \(t=12/5\), so the same relation would hold among the four exact rational points listed in Appendix A. Appendix B proves that those four points are independent. Therefore the assumed nontrivial relation cannot exist, and the four generic sections are independent.

The computation therefore proves the independence required for the generic rank lower bound. It does not compute the exact generic rank, and it does not give a rank upper bound for any of the six families or for the remaining fourteen triple classes.
