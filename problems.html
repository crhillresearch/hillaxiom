Unsolved problems

A thematic index of questions still open in Andrej Dujella’s Open Problems on Diophantine m-tuples and Elliptic Curves. Browse by mathematical interest or search the problem statements. The source document is updated regularly; this page reflects the version dated November 13, 2026.

High rank and elliptic curves

Rank records, torsion constraints, and ranks in families.

2.1Are ranks of elliptic curves over ℚ unbounded?

Do elliptic curves over ℚ exist with arbitrarily large rank? The PDF reports a rank lower bound of 32 as the current record.

rank boundsover ℚ

3.5Can a curve induced by a rational Diophantine triple have rank greater than 12?

Several such curves of rank 12 are known.

rank recordtriples

3.6Can a family induced by rational Diophantine triples have rank greater than 6 over ℚ(t)?

Rank 6 is known for this kind of elliptic curve over the rational function field.

familiesfunction fields

3.10Can a curve induced by a rational Diophantine quadruple have rank greater than 10?

A rank 11 example has been proposed as a solution, conditional on an auxiliary curve having positive rank. The PDF notes that the unconditional argument relies on a proposed converse theorem not yet independently established.

rank recordproposed solution

2.2Can ℚ-torsion ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/8ℤ occur with rank 4?

Rank 3 examples are known.

torsionrank 4

2.3Are there infinitely many curves with specified torsion and rank at least 2?

For torsion ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/8ℤ, and separately for ℤ/9ℤ, ℤ/10ℤ, and ℤ/12ℤ, are there infinitely many elliptic curves over ℚ of rank at least 2?

infinite familiestorsion

2.4(b)Are infinitely many curves with torsion ℤ/8ℤ of rank at least 4?

Infinitely many rank-at-least-3 examples are known.

infinite familiesrank 4

2.5What heuristic predicts rank parity in the known high-rank torsion families?

Seek a heuristic for parity distributions among known rank-at-least-3 families with torsion ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/6ℤ and, respectively, ℤ/8ℤ.

rank parityheuristics

2.6(b)Are infinitely many curves with torsion ℤ/6ℤ of rank at least 6?

Infinitely many examples of rank at least 5 are known.

infinite familiesrank 6

2.7Is there a genus-one-parametrized family with torsion ℤ/4ℤ and rank at least 7?

A curve over ℚ(t) with this torsion and rank 6 is known.

familiesrank 7

2.8Can quadratic-field torsion ℤ/13ℤ, ℤ/15ℤ, or ℤ/18ℤ occur with rank at least 3?

For each of these three admissible torsion groups, find an elliptic curve over a quadratic field with rank at least 3.

quadratic fieldstorsion

3.7Are infinitely many rank-zero curves induced by positive rational triples?

At least one such elliptic curve is known.

rank 0infinite families

3.13Is every positive-rank curve with torsion ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/6ℤ induced by a rational triple?

Equivalently, does there exist a positive-rank curve with this torsion subgroup that is not induced by a rational Diophantine triple?

torsionclassification

Diophantine tuples and square conditions

Extensions, exceptional configurations, and simultaneous square conditions.

1.3Must a certain identity hold for every integer extension of a D(−1)-triple?

Given a D(−1)-triple {a,b,c} and positive integer d such that ad+1, bd+1, cd+1 are squares, is (d+a+b−c)² = 4(ab−1)(cd+1)? It is known for several special families.

D(−1)identity

1.4Is K₃,₃ a Diophantine graph?

Do there exist six distinct positive integers a₁,a₂,a₃,b₁,b₂,b₃ such that every aᵢbⱼ+1 is a square?

Diophantine graphsintegers

1.5Can three linear factors yield a square for six positive integers?

Do distinct positive integers a₁,a₂,a₃ exist for which (a₁x+1)(a₂x+1)(a₃x+1) is a square for at least six positive integer x? Five-solution examples are known.

integer solutionssquare values

1.15Does a strong rational Diophantine quadruple exist?

Can four nonzero rationals aᵢ be chosen so that every aᵢaⱼ+1, including the diagonal cases, is a square?

rational tuplesstrong tuples

3.1Is there a rational Diophantine sextuple with at least two integer elements?

Sextuples with one integer element are known.

sextuplesintegral elements

3.2Does a rational Diophantine septuple exist?

Find seven distinct rationals for which each pairwise product plus one is a square.

septuplesrational tuples

3.4Can one give an explicit absolute bound for rational Diophantine tuple size?

Assume plausible conjectures and seek a numerical bound. Lang’s conjecture implies a bound but not an effective numerical one in the requested form.

boundsrational tuples

3.11Can a rational Diophantine sextuple contain a strong triple?

Sextuples containing a strong pair are known in infinite families.

sextuplesstrong tuples

3.12Are there infinitely many pairs of sextuples sharing a triple with square denominators?

All elements in both sextuples must have perfect-square denominators in lowest terms. Two pairs are known.

sextuplesdenominators

5.16Is there a rational Diophantine quintuple whose elements are all squares?

Seek five rational squares whose pairwise products plus one are squares.

quintuplessquares

5.18Is (5,7,24) the only integer triple satisfying the stated five square conditions?

For 1<a<b<c, require c+1, ab+1, ac+1, bc+1, and abc+1 all to be squares.

integer triplessquare conditions

Diophantine m-tuples: bounds, existence, and arithmetic

Questions about D(n)-sets, larger tuples, and simultaneous D(n) properties.

1.6Can one prove an unconditional absolute bound for perfect-power pair sets?

Bound the size of a set of positive integers where every pairwise product of distinct elements is one less than a perfect power. The result is known assuming abc.

perfect powersabc

1.7(d)Is every D(n)-quadruple finite in number when n is not a square?

The PDF’s asymptotic questions for pairs and triples and its square-n quadruple question are marked solved; this non-square case remains open.

countingD(n)

1.8Which z in ℤ[√−2] admit a D(z)-quadruple?

Characterize the ring elements for which four elements have pairwise products plus z equal to squares; partial results are known.

quadratic ringsclassification

1.9How does D(n)-quadruple existence relate to differences of squares in rings?

Formulate a conjecture covering arbitrary commutative rings with unity and agreeing with known examples and theorems.

commutative ringsconjecture

1.11Can an imaginary quadratic integer ring contain a D(1)-quintuple?

Every such D(1)-tuple has at most five elements, so the remaining question is whether the upper bound is attained.

quadratic fieldsquintuples

5.1Do D(n)-quadruples exist for n = −3, 3, 5, 8, 12, or 20?

Determine existence separately for each listed value of n.

existenceD(n)

5.2Are there only finitely many n with at most two D(n)-quadruples?

Consider integer n not congruent to 2 modulo 4.

countingD(n)

5.3(a)Are there other polynomial D((4k−1)(4k+1))-quadruples?

Exclude those obtained from the two known polynomial D(4k+3)-quadruples.

polynomial tuplesD(n)

5.3(b)Can a specified prime-constrained n have more than two D(n)-quadruples?

Here n=(4k−1)(4k+1), with the seven associated numbers listed in the source all prime.

prime valuesD(n)

5.4Can D(n)-tuple size be bounded by c log log |n|?

Prove such an upper bound for nonzero integer n with an absolute constant c.

size boundsD(n)

5.5Do third-power or fourth-power Diophantine quadruples exist?

Determine existence for either exponent.

perfect powersquadruples

5.10Is there a rational D(q)-sextuple for nonsquare q?

Find six rational elements whose pairwise products plus q are squares.

sextuplesrational tuples

5.11Can an integer D(n)-sextuple have all elements odd?

Odd-element D(n)-quintuples are known.

sextuplesparity

5.12Are there infinitely many triples that are also D(nᵢ)-triples for 1<n₂<n₃<n₄?

Several examples are known; the question asks whether there are infinitely many such triples.

simultaneous D(n)infinite families

5.13(b)How does the smallest shared D(n) parameter grow with the number of parameters?

For n₁(k), the least absolute nonzero integer among a set of k values for which one fixed triple is D(n) for every value, describe asymptotic behavior under plausible conjectures.

asymptoticsD(n)

5.14Can one four-element set be D(n)-quadruple for three distinct n?

All four elements must be nonzero integers.

simultaneous D(n)quadruples

5.15Can one five-element set be D(n)-quintuple for two distinct n?

All five elements must be nonzero integers.

simultaneous D(n)quintuples

Pell equations and integer points

Integral solutions, regularity, and arithmetic structure.

4.1Does a Pell-type equation have at most one small positive solution?

For k≥2, does x²−(k²+1)y²=k² have at most one solution with 0<y<k−1 and x>0? It is proved for several infinite classes and for odd k with at most three prime divisors.

Pell equationsuniqueness

4.2Can another parameter yield more than four fundamental solutions?

For x²−(k²−1)y²=2−k², is there a positive k other than 33539 with more than four fundamental solutions?

Pell equationssolutions

4.5Can all entries of a Diophantine quadruple be congruent modulo k≥3?

Determine whether a quadruple can satisfy a≡b≡c≡d (mod k).

congruencesquadruples

4.6Are all Diophantine quadruples regular?

Only finitely many irregular quadruples can exist, with explicit bounds on their larger entries; the conjecture is reduced to a finite problem.

regularityquadruples

4.7Are all D(4)-quadruples regular?

This is closely linked to the regularity question for ordinary Diophantine quadruples.

regularityD(4)

4.9Are the listed points all integer points on this elliptic curve family?

For k≥3, determine whether the only integral points on y²=((k−1)x+1)((k+1)x+1)(4kx+1) are (0,±1) and (16k³−4k, ±(128k⁶−112k⁴+20k²−1)). Known for k≤5000 and some additional cases.

integer pointselliptic curves
